## Why the assessments changed

**Buffy:** the ensemble still has substantial value, and the camera score rises because formal execution deserves separate credit. The reductions concern inconsistent constraints, uneven progress and payoff preparation across the whole run. Outstanding episodes cannot certify seven seasons. The assessment balances the visual case in [season-five criticism](https://www.dvdtalk.com/reviews/8523/buffy-the-vampire-slayer-the-complete-fifth-season/) with the strengths and causal weaknesses of [the final episode](https://thebuffyrewatch.wordpress.com/2014/04/08/robins-review-s7-e22-chosen/). Its supernatural premise is not a deduction.

**The Walking Dead:** the greatest losses are in drive and resolution. Recurrent delay and incomplete central obligations matter more under this rubric than endurance or a large cast. Physical staging retains a strong score; [directors' craft accounts](https://www.dga.org/craft/dgaq/issues/1502-spring-2015/on-the-air-the-walking-dead) support that independent strength. The [complete-series retrospective](https://www.theringer.com/2022/11/21/tv/the-walking-dead-series-finale-season-11-zombies-spinoffs) and [finale analysis](https://www.denofgeek.com/tv/the-walking-dead-season-11-episode-24-review-rest-in-peace/) inform the whole-run judgment. Spin-off resolutions are outside scope.

**Boston Legal:** character breadth, institutional credibility and accumulated development score below the old blanket praise. The central relationship's resolution rises instead of being dragged down with them. [Opening-season analysis](https://www.dvdreview.com/2006/05/boston-legal-season-one/) and [final-season coverage](https://sitcomsonline.com/blog/2009/05/blog-dvd-review-boston-legal-season.html) support that distinction. The purpose is to judge what the speeches and relationships accomplish, not how forcefully they announce a position.

**The Practice:** procedural obligations and financial pressure remain credible strengths. The weaker whole-run continuity and uneven distribution of concluding attention lower the writing assessment. Evidence spans [early-run criticism](https://www.dvdtalk.com/reviews/28802/practice-volume-one-the/), [the final season](https://mediamikes.com/2014/04/dvd-review-the-practice-the-final-season/) and [the final episode's scope](https://thetvdb.com/series/the-practice/episodes/110291). Middle-season and camera coverage remain relatively thin; uncertainty is disclosed rather than converted into a quality penalty.

**Shameless (US):** the principal performances retain an exceptional score. The largest deduction concerns repeated crises that do not accumulate enough meaning across the complete run. This does not demand steady personal improvement from characters facing addiction or poverty. The [whole-series retrospective](https://dvdcorner.net/2025/03/28/shameless-the-complete-series-dvd-review/) and [John Wells's finale interview](https://www.tvline.com/interviews/shameless-recap-season-11-episode-12-series-finale-frank-dies-1234659006/) inform the distinction between an appropriate open future and weak follow-through.

**Fleabag:** small rooms and a short run previously received too little credit. The revised reasons identify psychological depth, purposeful framing, progression and earned resolution, while keeping the narrower world and spectacle scores modest. [Tony Miller's account of the camera work](https://www.motionpictures.org/2019/08/fleabags-emmy-nominated-cinematographer-on-crafting-a-nearly-flawless-second-season-2/) and [the second-series analysis](https://www.theringer.com/2019/05/20/tv/fleabag-season-2) support those distinctions. The old perfect scores are not reinstated.

**I May Destroy You:** contradictory characters, interacting ethical questions and formal completion deserve more weight than a demand for conventional closure. The final judgment distinguishes an earned ambiguous ending from a plot left unfinished. [Production and performance reporting](https://www.backstage.com/uk/magazine/article/how-i-may-destroy-you-got-made-73447/) and [ending analysis](https://time.com/5882412/i-may-destroy-you-ending-explained/) inform the assessment. Awards play no role in the numbers.

**The Pitt:** the largest correction. The old review's first-season discussion could not explain a record covering thirty episodes. The new assessment covers both completed seasons, evaluating the hospital's constraints, ensemble behaviour and spatial clarity. [Johanna Coelho's camera account](https://www.fdtimes.com/2025/01/27/johanna-coelho-the-pitt/), [season-two criticism](https://au.variety.com/2026/tv/reviews/the-pitt-season-2-review-hbo-max-31624/) and [the second-season finale assessment](https://www.theatlantic.com/culture/2026/04/the-pitt-season-2-finale-robby/686838/) supply separate coverage. Continuing beyond that endpoint is not itself a resolution penalty.

**Fallout:** the revised score credits what the first season establishes and pays off, including its contrasting leads and material world. It retains deductions for simplified motivations and convenient plotting. [The cast's account](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/entertainment/fallout-tv-series-cast) and [episode-specific finale analysis](https://www.episodicmedium.tv/review-fallout-the-beginning-season/) support the season-one scope. Later seasons and fidelity to the games do not determine these ratings.

## Provenance and limits

These are provisional research assessments. The complete runs were not watched for this pass. Every record states the extent and limitations of its evidence; source coverage is strongest around documented examples and endpoints. Production interviews describe intentions and methods, which do not by themselves prove artistic success. The half-point boundaries are reasoned judgments, not measurements with statistical precision.

All nine records advance to assessment version 2. The previous ratings, scope and every historical dimension excerpt remain preserved. The seven numerical inputs in the compatibility catalogue match the live catalogue. Episode counts, formula, account preferences and the other 433 preserved records are unchanged.

Five newly superseded reviews are withheld because of specific factual or scope problems. Their archive pages remain preserved with corrective notices, are marked noindex and are omitted from the sitemap. The other four remain accessible as clearly superseded commentary. The new assessments and their seven reasons remain visible in either case; none of these programmes is removed from ranking.

The remaining audit candidates, including Andor, Severance, Girls and Watchmen, have not been silently rescored. The high-ranking reference works also need the same calibration. Neither familiar prestige nor Josh's preferred genre exempts an entry from scrutiny.

## Follow-up source audit

The placement of I May Destroy You at 15 needs stronger comparative support than the first pass provided. Its six listed sources are not six independent evaluations of artistic quality. The two principal critical articles, TIME's [series assessment](https://time.com/5844881/i-may-destroy-you-hbo-review/) and [ending analysis](https://time.com/5882412/i-may-destroy-you-ending-explained/), are both by Judy Berman. The other four establish programme identity, production context, episode events and an awards correction. Those have useful but different evidential roles. This is a source-selection limitation; it is not evidence that Berman's observations are false or that an audience manipulated ratings.

Independent criticism supplies both support and reservations. Kate Stables's [Sight and Sound assessment](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/reviews/i-may-destroy-you-review-michaela-coel-rewrites-rules-game) identifies accomplished performances and narrative experimentation alongside a less disciplined middle and blunt satire. Sonia Saraiya's [Vanity Fair review](https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2020/06/michaela-coel-i-may-destroy-you-hbo-review) similarly distinguishes the compelling central performance from structural unevenness; it is an early review, not evidence about the completed ending. These are arguments to examine, not votes to average. Praise for a subject's social significance supplies no craft points.

The current reasoning is least secure where it moves from an observed quality to an exceptional numerical grade without enough comparison. Character contradictions support depth but do not alone establish 8.5 against other ensembles. Several related ethical questions do not alone establish 8.5 for conceptual density. A purposeful nonlinear structure does not alone establish 8 for narrative drive. The next comparative assessment must show the scene-level progression, independent development of ideas and strength of the substantial supporting roles that justify those boundaries.

The existing arithmetic illustrates the calibration problem. With default weights and length off, I May Destroy You scores 8.10 and Chernobyl 8.08. Their character inputs are 8.5 and 7 respectively; the latter is still a legacy judgment. That large input gap has not received a matched assessment. A hypothetical half-point decrease in I May Destroy You's character input alone gives 7.98 and position 26 in this catalogue. This is sensitivity analysis, not a proposed rescore or a confidence interval.

No ratings, weights or rank rules change in this follow-up. The remaining question concerns evidence for the judgments and comparisons, not a malfunction in the calculator. The revised [source and audience-affinity standard](ASSESSMENTS.md#audience-affinity-messages-and-source-independence) requires concrete craft evidence, independent critical coverage and consistent benchmarks across viewpoints. The claim that a particular ideological audience inflated this show's reception remains unestablished.
